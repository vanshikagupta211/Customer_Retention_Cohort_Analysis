# Customer Retention & Cohort Analysis

## 📌 Project Overview

Customer retention is a critical business metric because retaining existing customers can significantly contribute to long-term revenue and business growth.

This end-to-end data analytics project analyzes transaction-level retail data to understand customer purchasing behavior, retention patterns, cohort performance, revenue contribution, product performance, purchase frequency, and geographic sales trends.

The project combines **SQL Server, Python, and Power BI** to demonstrate a complete analytics workflow:

**Data Profiling → Data Cleaning → Exploratory Analysis → Cohort Analysis → Business Analysis → Interactive Dashboard Development → Business Insights**

---

## 🎯 Business Objective

The primary objective of this project is to understand customer retention and purchasing behavior and identify the factors that contribute to revenue.

The analysis addresses key business questions such as:

- How many customers are repeat customers?
- What percentage of customers return after their first purchase?
- How does customer retention change over time?
- Which customer cohorts demonstrate stronger retention?
- How much revenue is generated by repeat customers?
- Which customers generate the highest revenue?
- Which products generate the highest revenue and unit sales?
- How frequently do customers place orders?
- Which countries contribute the most revenue and customers?
- How do revenue and Average Order Value change over time?
- Which customer segments should receive greater retention focus?

🐍 Python Analysis
Python was used to independently clean the raw UCI dataset and perform exploratory, customer, revenue, and cohort analysis.

Python Data Cleaning
The following cleaning logic was applied:

df_clean = df.copy()

df_clean = df_clean.dropna(subset=['CustomerID'])
df_clean = df_clean.drop_duplicates()

df_clean = df_clean[
    (df_clean['Quantity'] > 0) &
    (df_clean['UnitPrice'] > 0)
]

df_clean['CustomerID'] = df_clean['CustomerID'].astype(int)

df_clean['Revenue'] = (
    df_clean['Quantity'] *
    df_clean['UnitPrice']
)

Python Cleaned Dataset
After Python-based cleaning:
- 392,692 transaction rows
- 4,338 unique customers
- No remaining missing values in the cleaned analytical dataset
- No duplicate rows
- Revenue calculated at transaction level

SQL and Python cleaning were intentionally performed as independent analytical workflows. The resulting cleaned row counts differ because the cleaning and exclusion logic was implemented separately in each environment.

📅 Cohort Analysis
Cohort analysis was performed to understand customer retention over time.
Each customer was assigned to a cohort based on the month of their first purchase.

Cohort Definition
Cohort Month = Customer's First Purchase Month

Cohort Index
The cohort index represents the number of months since the customer's first purchase.
Cohort Index 1 → First Purchase Month
Cohort Index 2 → Month After First Purchase
Cohort Index 3 → Two Months After First Purchase
...

Retention Calculation
Retention Rate =
Customers Active in Month N
/
Customers in Original Cohort
× 100

The analysis produced a cohort retention matrix and heatmap to identify retention patterns across different customer acquisition cohorts.

👥 Customer Purchase Frequency
Customer purchase frequency was analyzed using the number of distinct invoices/orders associated with each customer.
Key observations include:
- Most customers place relatively few orders.
- A smaller group of customers places significantly more orders.
- The distribution shows a strong long-tail pattern.
- The highest observed customer purchase frequency was 209 distinct orders.
This highlights the presence of highly engaged customers who contribute significantly to repeat purchasing behavior.

💡 Business Recommendations
Based on the analysis, several business actions can be considered.

1. Improve First-to-Second Purchase Conversion
Customer retention drops significantly after the initial purchase.
Potential strategies include:
- Personalized follow-up campaigns
- Second-purchase incentives
- Product recommendations
- Email reminders
- Targeted promotional offers

2. Focus on Repeat Customers
Repeat customers generate the majority of revenue.
Businesses should consider:
- Loyalty programs
- Personalized offers
- Customer engagement campaigns
- Repeat-purchase incentives
- Retention-focused marketing

3. Identify and Retain High-Value Customers
A relatively small number of customers contribute substantial revenue.
Potential strategies include:
- VIP customer programs
- Exclusive offers
- Personalized recommendations
- Early access to products
- Dedicated customer engagement

4. Monitor Cohort Performance
Cohort analysis can help businesses understand whether newer customer groups are becoming more or less engaged over time.
Tracking retention by cohort can support:
- Marketing performance evaluation
- Customer acquisition analysis
- Retention strategy optimization
- Campaign comparison

5. Analyze Geographic Opportunities
The United Kingdom dominates the dataset, while other countries represent smaller customer bases.
Businesses could use country-level analysis to identify opportunities for:
- Market expansion
- Localized marketing
- Regional promotions
- Customer acquisition strategies
